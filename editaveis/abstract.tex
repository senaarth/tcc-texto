\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}

With the advancement of continuous delivery practices influenced by the \textit{Lean Startup} methodology, software companies have been shortening their development cycles, releasing new versions of their products at an increasingly rapid pace. In many organizations, the user feedback process is often slow and unsystematic, making it difficult to accurately assess the value delivered by the developed features. In this context, the practice of Continuous Experimentation is becoming a standard in large companies, utilizing controlled experiments to validate products based on empirical usage data. This helps product managers make data-driven decisions, ensuring the quality and alignment of the software with users' actual needs. In this context, this study aimed to identify the activities and techniques required to systematize a Data-Driven Development process. Basing on this understanding, a case study was designed and conducted within a private Brazilian organization. The goal was to apply this process and evaluate its effectiveness in supporting decision-making between different versions of a software product. The findings from the case study are presented, highlighting the performance of the proposed process, along with its limitations, threats to validity, and opportunities for future research.

\vspace{\onelineskip}

\noindent
\textbf{Keywords}: online controlled experiments, continuous experimentation, data-driven development, data analysis, software quality.

 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
